A high-level systems assessment looks at whether the technical direction of a platform still matches the organisation’s long-term business goals.

This kind of work usually becomes useful when teams have had the freedom to make local decisions, but the structures needed to keep the platform coherent have not kept up. The assessment is a chance to step back, look at the platform as a whole, and find where alignment, architectural consistency, or strategy would help you move forward more effectively.

## What a High-Level Systems Assessment Can Support

There are several situations where this is a useful first step.

### Strategic Technical Direction

When the business has clear long-term objectives but no defined technical path to get there, an assessment can provide that clarity.

That means discovery and analysis of the current platform architecture, then a gap analysis of where today’s landscape differs from what those goals require.

You leave with a structured report covering:
- Opportunities for architectural improvement
- Areas of technical risk
- Strategic options for evolving the platform

That can then inform later technical work, a deeper architectural review, or a wider programme of change.

### Complex or Large-Scale Projects

For large or technically complex initiatives that touch several systems or teams, starting with a high-level view of the platform usually leads to better decisions.

A clear picture of architecture, dependencies, and risk early on reduces the chance of expensive redesign later.

## Engagement Format

Work starts with a scoping conversation about the current platform and what you are trying to achieve.

That discussion typically covers:
- The number of deployed services or systems
- The approximate size and structure of engineering teams
- Key business objectives for the platform
- The outcomes you hope to get from the engagement

From there, we propose a structure and an indicative timeline.

Each engagement is tailored, but a typical shape is:

#### Phase 1: Discovery & Assessment
A focused discovery phase to understand the business context, current architecture, and how the organisation is set up.

#### Phase 2: Gap Analysis Report
A structured look at the current landscape, highlighting architectural gaps, technical risks, and opportunities for improvement.

#### Phase 3: Strategy Proposal
Turning those findings into a technical strategy that lines up with your business goals.

#### Phase 4: Roadmap & Target Architectures
A high-level roadmap, and any short-term target architectures needed to support it.

#### Phase 5: Handover
A structured handover so internal teams can take the work forward with confidence.

## Example Expected Outcomes

Specific deliverables for each phase are agreed at the start.

They may include:

- Platform-wide architectural gap analysis
- A report covering improvements and technical risk
- Cost/value analysis of potential architectural changes
- High-level plans and a roadmap aligned with business goals
